The Batch

Andrew Ng · DeepLearning.AI — Weekly Research Framing

♥ Play

The Batch does something rare in AI media: it explains without dumbing down or hyping anything up. Andrew Ng's weekly letter closes each issue, a rare window into how one of the field's most credentialed educators is actually thinking week to week, not just what shipped.

Card 03 63words

Ahead of AI

Sebastian Raschka · Open-Source LLM Deep Dives

♥ Play

Sebastian Raschka does not summarize papers he has not run himself. Ahead of AI walks through fine-tuning, LoRA, and evaluation with a textbook's rigor and a blog's cadence, because Raschka tests the code before writing a single word about it. If you train or deploy your own open-source models for a living, this newsletter respects your time enough to actually show its work.

Read Ahead of AI ↗
Card 04 81words

Import AI

Jack Clark · AI Policy & Compute Strategy

♦ Not Yet

Jack Clark has written Import AI since 2016, before most of this list's newsletters existed, and it shows in the depth. Each issue pairs paper summaries with analysis of compute trends, national strategy, and governance, written by an Anthropic co-founder who sees policy from inside the room, not the press gallery. He closes every issue with a short story imagining where this all goes. It is not a five-minute read. Save it for when you have the twenty minutes it deserves.

Read Import AI ↗
Card 05 95words

Ben's Bites

Ben Tossell · Startup & Builder Ecosystem

♥ Play

Ben Tossell built the first AI daily newsletter back in October 2022, months before the boom made everyone else try hard to copy the format. Ben's Bites now reaches over a hundred thousand subscribers daily with rapid curation of new tools, product launches, and startups an exited founder turned investor would genuinely want sitting right there on his own desk. It skews toward what is actually shipping right now over what is theorized, which makes it less useful for research depth, and more useful if you are the one building the thing, watching competitors move.

Read Ben's Bites ↗
Card 06 108words

Latent Space

Swyx & Alessio · AI Engineer Newsletter & Podcast

♥ Play

Swyx and Alessio built Latent Space to bridge traditional software engineering and machine learning research in a way most AI newsletters do not even attempt, because most of them are written by people who have never actually shipped the systems they spend their time describing in public. The essays assume you can read code, and the companion podcast interviews the engineers actually building retrieval pipelines, agent frameworks, and the vector infrastructure sitting underneath all of it. If you are a software engineer pivoting into AI, or already building agentic systems in production, this is the rare publication willing to go several layers deeper than the vendor talking points.

Read Latent Space ↗
Card 07 126words

The Median

The DataCamp Team · Data & AI Literacy Briefing

♦ Not Yet

The Median has a structural advantage and a structural conflict, and it is worth naming both plainly. Published by DataCamp's own editorial team, it pairs the week's most important AI and data developments with practical context: what changed, why it matters, and what to actually go learn, complete with links to tutorials and courses. That is exactly why it earns a place on this list, and exactly why it deserves a second look before you trust it completely — a newsletter owned by a course platform has every incentive to conclude the answer to this week's news is always: go take a course. The signal here is real. So is the funnel underneath it. Read it for the former, keep your own judgment for the latter.
